## Section IV (setup): added paragraph after the "two families of experiments" paragraph

> **Scoring.** Each item is scored against its expected behavior. Answer-expected
> items (clean, conflict, context_noise, chunk_boundary, evidence_position) are scored
> on answer correctness: an item is correct if any of exact match, token-F1 ≥ 0.6
> or the LLM-judge verdict accepts it (Table XIX gives each signal alone; the judge
> carries most of the decision, and the lexical signals add 1.5–3.5 points on clean).
> Abstain-expected items (missing_evidence, hard_negative) are scored on correct
> abstention. A condition's drop is *paired*: every failure case is compared with
> its own parent clean seed, not with the clean-set mean, so the drop is not
> confounded by which seeds an operator could be built from. The Failure Robustness
> Score is FRS = 1 − mean paired drop over the six operators. The correctness judge
> (DeepSeek-V3.2) is outside the evaluated set; the pipeline refuses to run if the
> judge is also an evaluated model. Calls that fail after retries are excluded and
> counted, never scored as wrong; the final runs have 0 failed calls.
>
> **Fixed context budget.** Every clean and failure case presents exactly eight
> passages. Operators that produce fewer (e.g. missing_evidence keeps only the
> passages left after removal; chunk_boundary uses three to five) are padded with
> off-topic passages from other pages, drawn with a per-case seed and checked not to
> contain any surface form of the answer. A paired drop therefore reflects what an
> operator changes, not a shorter or longer context than the parent clean case.
>
> **Uncertainty.** Besides mean ± std over the three seeded runs, we report 95%
> bootstrap CIs pooled over the runs (2,000 resamples). The resampling unit is the
> parent clean seed (600 clusters), so all failure variants of a seed move together,
> and all models are resampled on the same clusters, which makes model-vs-model FRS
> differences paired.

## Conflict operator: replaces its description in Section III / Table II

> **conflict.** The gold chunk is kept, and a second copy of the *same* chunk is
> inserted with the answer span replaced by a plausible alternate. The conflicting
> passage therefore matches the gold passage in length, style and surrounding facts,
> and differs only in the answer. Alternates are chosen in this order:
> (i) for dates and numbers, a small perturbation of the gold value;
> (ii) otherwise, an alternate written by the generator LLM. It must not overlap the
> gold answer, must not already appear in the passage, and is rejected by the
> independent judge if it is judged correct;
> (iii) as a fallback, a same-type entity drawn from other seeds' answers.
> Across the three runs these are 36%, 58% and 7% of conflict cases. Expected behavior:
> answer with the gold value or flag the conflict. Severity is the number of
> conflicting copies and their position.

## missing_evidence / hard_negative: replaces their description in Section III / Table II

> **missing_evidence.** All evidence for the answer is removed; expected behavior is to
> abstain. Severity is how close the remaining context is to the removed evidence:
> *low* keeps only passages from other pages, *medium* keeps neighboring chunks of the
> source page, and *high* keeps the gold chunk with the supporting sentence deleted.
> **hard_negative.** The gold chunk is replaced by topically similar passages that do not
> contain the answer. For both operators, a case is rejected if any passage still
> contains the gold answer in any surface form (bracket, date-order, comma-head and
> type-suffix variants, matched on whole words). The generator-independent LLM judge
> then checks every remaining case, and the case is rejected if the judge can still
> answer from the contexts. Across the three runs the judge rejects 12–16% of
> high-severity missing_evidence and 2–5% of hard_negative cases that pass the string
> checks; no abstain-expected case is left unaudited.

> Five findings follow from Tables XII, XIII and XV–XVII. First, the absence operators
> work as intended: the three models abstain on 93–95% of missing_evidence and 96% of
> hard_negative cases. Of the remaining answers, only 0–2% of cases are correct
> (mostly seeds the model answers closed-book: 1.9% vs 0.9% on missing_evidence), and
> 4–6% are hallucinated. The rate of failing to abstain rises with how close the
> remaining context is to the removed evidence: 0.2, 5.7 and 13.2 points at low, medium
> and high severity (Table XVI). Second, context_noise, chunk_boundary and
> evidence_position are weak stressors at pilot severities: paired drops are at most
> 4.4 points, and for context_noise and evidence_position every model's 95% CI
> includes zero. chunk_boundary grows with severity (−0.1 / 2.1 / 5.4 points) and is
> the one of the three that reliably hurts gpt-oss (4.4 points, CI 2.9–5.9). With a
> fixed budget of eight passages, these models show neither distractor sensitivity
> nor positional bias.
> Third, conflict is the dominant failure for every model: paired drops are 23.4 ±
> 2.3 (nova-pro), 23.3 ± 0.8 (llama) and 32.1 ± 0.7 (gpt-oss) points, and gpt-oss's
> excess over the other two is outside the CIs. In 18–28% of conflict cases the model
> commits to the planted alternate without noting the contradiction. Fourth, how a
> model fails conflict depends on the model, not only on the operator. Under the
> default "shortest exact answer" prompt, llama names both values in 37% of cases,
> while nova-pro and gpt-oss pick one value (gold in 62% and 52%). A judge that
> requires a single answer marks many of llama's two-value answers wrong, so llama
> ranks last on judge-only accuracy (0.61) yet ties nova-pro for the best handled rate
> (0.75 vs 0.74). For this reason we score conflict by outcome rather than by the
> judge alone. Fifth, conflict behavior depends on the prompt and on prior knowledge.
> When the prompt permits flagging a conflict, acknowledgement rises from 11–38% to
> 54–84% and the handled rate reaches 0.87–0.95. Seeds the model can already answer
> closed-book are handled 9–17 points more often than seeds it cannot (e.g. 85.0% vs
> 68.9% for nova-pro). Part of the default-prompt conflict accuracy is therefore
> parametric recall, not resolution of the retrieved evidence.

## Section VI.B (failure insights): replaces the conflict part

> The larger, more actionable insight is conflict. Gold evidence remains present, in
> full, next to a passage identical except for the answer. Even so, all three models
> lose 23–32 points, and in roughly a fifth to a quarter of cases they adopt the planted
> value silently. Two controls separate this from artifacts of the operator. A
> closed-book probe shows the models recall 30–48% of the answers without context, and
> those seeds are resolved more often. A conflict-aware prompt shows the models *can*
> detect the contradiction (acknowledgement 54–84%), but the usual "give the shortest answer"
> instruction suppresses it. For a deployed RAG system, this means a stale but
> not-yet-purged document is not something the model can be relied on to adjudicate.
> Whether the contradiction is surfaced depends on the answer-format instruction, and
> which value is chosen depends partly on what the model already believes.

## Section V.H (new): the regression loop on two corpus snapshots

> **Setup.** Snapshot A is the pilot corpus (768 Wikipedia pages fetched on
> 2026-08-01). Snapshot B re-fetches the same pages on 2026-10-28; revision ids are
> recorded, so both snapshots are reproducible. In three months, 580 of 768 pages have
> a new revision and 4 no longer resolve. Of A's 12,366 chunks, 11,296 are unchanged;
> B has 13,599 chunks. Of the 600 clean seeds built on A, 4.0–6.5% no longer hold on B:
> 28 have an edited supporting sentence and 3 have lost the gold answer entirely (e.g.
> a CEO question whose answer changed). A static benchmark therefore goes stale at
> about 1.3–2.2% of seeds per month on this corpus.
>
> **Regeneration.** We rebuilt the benchmark from snapshot B with the unchanged
> pipeline and configuration (seed 42). After fetching and chunking, regeneration took 14 minutes of wall time
> (generation 4.1 min, validation 2.3, seed selection < 1 s, conflict alternates 2.6,
> injection with judge audit 5.1) and 3,736 logged generator / judge calls (1.8M tokens; injection-judge calls are not logged),
> excluding the 3-model evaluation (23,188 calls including judge calls, 13.6M tokens,
> 2.8 h at the gateway's concurrency limit of 10). Ingest is not the bottleneck: a
> timed re-fetch of the 768 pages (764 resolve, 11 MB of wikitext) takes 188 s at the
> configured 8 requests/s API limit (4.1 pages/s), and chunking them into 13.6k chunks
> takes 16 s on one CPU core. End to end, a regeneration is about 18 minutes and 3.7k
> LLM calls; its cost is dominated by the evaluation, which scales with
> models × cases, not with corpus size. The funnel reproduces: 1,000
> candidates, 369 accepted, 200 seeds, 3,429 verified failure cases (171 quarantined,
> 0 unaudited). Only 29 of B's 200 questions coincide with A's, so A and B are two
> independent draws of the benchmark.
>
> **Gate.** For each model × condition the gate compares a candidate run with a
> baseline run. It reports a FAIL when the expected-behavior score drops by more than
> 5 points and the 95% bootstrap CI of the drop (2,000 resamples) lies below zero. The
> comparison is paired by item when both runs share a benchmark and unpaired when it
> was regenerated. We run two comparisons (Table XVIII):
>
> 1. *Knowledge update, same RAG system* (A → B, unpaired). Every one of the 21 cells
>    passes (largest drop 2.9 points; FRS 0.934/0.943/0.923 on A vs 0.945/0.953/0.921 on
>    B for nova-pro/llama/gpt-oss). Decision: SHIP. The corpus update does not by itself
>    degrade the system.
> 2. *Degraded retrieval configuration* (B, top-k 8 → 4, paired). The reader receives
>    only the first four of the eight passages. Clean accuracy is unchanged for every
>    model (|Δ| ≤ 1 point), because the gold passage is ranked first, so a clean-only
>    regression test would ship this change. The failure operators block it in 12 of 21
>    cells: evidence_position drops 64–65 points, context_noise 46–47, conflict 7.5–15.5
>    and chunk_boundary 7–9 (all CIs below zero). context_noise places the gold passage
>    uniformly over the eight slots and evidence_position at slot 1, 5 or 8 (by
>    severity), so a top-4 cut removes it in about half and two thirds of cases; the
>    scores (≈0.50 and ≈0.32) match exactly the evidence the configuration discards.
>    The pre-padding run showed a smaller context_noise drop (19–20 points) only
>    because its contexts were shorter and the gold passage more often sat in the
>    first four. FRS falls from
>    0.92–0.95 to 0.71–0.73. The abstain-expected operators are unaffected, as expected,
>    since truncation cannot add evidence. Decision: BLOCK.
>
> The second comparison is the case the benchmark is designed for. A configuration
> error that is invisible on clean questions is caught, and the operators that fail
> name the component: positional and distractor operators point to the
> retrieval-depth setting.

## Section III.C (validation): rule definitions and quality score (Reviewer #3)

> **Rule checks.** A candidate is rejected by any of the following deterministic
> checks. Matching is on normalized text (lower-cased, punctuation and articles
> removed) unless stated otherwise.
>
> | Rule | Rejects when | Example rejected | Rejected / run | Only this rule |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | title_leak_in_question | the source page title appears in the question | "What is the name of the river near which Colonel William Light chose to locate the city centre of Adelaide?" (page *Adelaide*) | 464.0 | 372.0 |
> | possible_multiple_answers (uniqueness) | a *who/which/what* question with a person or organization answer, whose supporting sentence lists entities joined by "and" that the gold answer does not | "Who shared the 1952 Nobel Prize in Physics with Felix Bloch…?" | 122.0 | 56.0 |
> | answer_not_in_supporting_sentence (containment) | the gold answer does not occur in the supporting sentence | "Which university awarded John Bardeen his Ph.D. in physics?" (sentence names no university) | 57.3 | 27.7 |
> | refers_to_passage | the question points at its source ("the passage", "the text", "mentioned") | "What is the population of Góra as of the year mentioned in the passage?" | 21.7 | 11.0 |
> | time_sensitive_phrasing | relative time ("current", "latest", "recently", an undated "as of") | "As of what date did Audacity have over 114.2 million downloads…?" | 17.7 | 8.7 |
> | answer_in_question | the gold answer occurs in the question | "What was the name of the settlement … captured by forces of the Demak Sultanate in 1527 and later renamed Jayakarta?" (gold: Jayakarta) | 17.3 | 7.0 |
> | unresolved_referent | a pronoun with no name before it, or a possessive / "the company"-style phrase in a question that names nothing | "What are the two main types of physicists and what do they specialize in?" | 17.0 | 9.7 |
> | supporting_sentence_not_in_chunk (containment) | the supporting sentence is not a substring of the source chunk | "What significant contribution did Néel make around 1930…?" (sentence not found in the cited chunk) | 8.7 | 5.7 |
> | length / empty | question < 12 characters or > 60 tokens; empty question or answer | — | 0 | 0 |
>
> Schema validation parses the generator's JSON against the CandidateQA schema
> (100% pass). Deduplication runs last over accepted candidates and drops a
> candidate whose normalized question, or normalized (supporting sentence, answer)
> pair, matches an earlier one exactly or has character-5-gram Jaccard similarity
> ≥ 0.85 (2.3 per run).
>
> **Quality score.** q = 0.30·[rules pass] + 0.10·[uniqueness passes]
> + 0.30·c·(a + s + u + k)/4 + 0.30·max(F1_baseline, [baseline correct]), where
> a, s, u, k are the judge's answerable / supported / unique / clear verdicts and
> c its confidence. In the released configuration the threshold of 0.85 is
> redundant: every candidate that passes the hard gates (rules, uniqueness, judge
> with confidence ≥ 0.6, baseline correct) scores ≥ 0.985, and any rule failure caps
> q at 0.70. Sweeping the threshold from 0 to 0.95 accepts exactly the same 366.3
> candidates per run. We therefore describe q as a ranking score reported with each
> seed, not as a separate filtering stage, and drop the "quality threshold" row from
> Table VII (its 575 rejections are all also rejected by another signal).

## Response-to-reviewers points (Reviewer #1)

- **Matched parent:** Confirmed, and caused by a stale resume. Generation resumed from a
  checkpoint whose chunks were no longer in the corpus, so some failure cases did not
  match their clean parent. The pipeline now drops stale checkpoints and refuses to
  select seeds from a stale candidate file. All three runs were regenerated from
  scratch, and drops are paired per parent seed.
- **Accuracy vs. abstention:** Abstain-expected operators are now scored on correct
  abstention, not answer accuracy. Previously, missing_evidence and hard_negative
  "accuracy" near zero was the desired behavior shown as a failure.
- **Self-judging:** The earlier judge silently fell back to nova-pro, an evaluated
  model. The judge is now DeepSeek-V3.2, and independence is enforced in code.
- **Silent failures:** Failed calls were scored as wrong answers. They are now excluded
  and counted (0 in the final runs).
- **Untested regeneration claim / no retriever:** Done as suggested: two Wikipedia
  revision snapshots three months apart plus one deliberately degraded retrieval
  configuration (top-k 8 → 4), run with the existing code (Section V.H). The corpus
  update passes the gate; the top-k change keeps clean accuracy unchanged and is
  blocked by four operators. Regeneration costs 14 minutes and about 3.7k LLM calls.
- **Leakage in abstain operators:** Answer surface variants are now matched, and an
  independent judge audits every abstain-expected case. This lowered the
  missing_evidence drop from 9–12 to 5–7 points and made its severity curve monotone.
- **Statistics:** Exact McNemar tests on identical items (Table XIX, Holm-corrected)
  confirm the gpt-oss conflict gap (p ≤ 1e-9 against both other models).
  Seeded-run tables report mean ± std over three runs, and Table XVII
  adds 95% cluster-bootstrap CIs (resampling parent seeds) for every paired drop, FRS
  and the pairwise FRS differences. nova-pro and llama are tied; gpt-oss is lower with
  a CI that excludes zero. The regression gate reports a bootstrap 95% CI per cell.
- **Judge validation:** A blind 270-answer sheet (15 per model × condition) is in
  `reviews/pilot_stability_s42/`. Cohen's κ is computed by `ragfailbench
  judge-agreement` once it is labeled (*pending*).
