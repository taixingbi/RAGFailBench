## Section IV (setup): added paragraph after the "two families of experiments" paragraph

> **Scoring.** Each item is scored against its expected behavior. Answer-expected
> items (clean, conflict, context_noise, chunk_boundary, evidence_position) are scored
> on answer correctness: exact match, token-F1 ≥ 0.6, or an LLM-judge verdict.
> Abstain-expected items (missing_evidence, hard_negative) are scored on correct
> abstention. A condition's drop is *paired*: every failure case is compared with
> its own parent clean seed, not with the clean-set mean, so the drop is not
> confounded by which seeds an operator could be built from. The Failure Robustness
> Score is FRS = 1 − mean paired drop over the six operators. The correctness judge
> (DeepSeek-V3.2) is outside the evaluated set; the pipeline refuses to run if the
> judge is also an evaluated model. Calls that fail after retries are excluded and
> counted, never scored as wrong; the final runs have 0 failed calls.

## Conflict operator: replaces its description in Section III / Table II

> **conflict.** The gold chunk is kept, and a second copy of the *same* chunk is
> inserted with the answer span replaced by a plausible alternate. The conflicting
> passage therefore matches the gold passage in length, style and surrounding facts,
> and differs only in the answer. Alternates are chosen in this order:
> (i) for dates and numbers, a small perturbation of the gold value;
> (ii) otherwise, an alternate written by the generator LLM. It must not overlap the
> gold answer, must not already appear in the passage, and is rejected by the
> independent judge if it is judged correct;
> (iii) as a fallback, a same-type entity drawn from other seeds' answers.
> Across the three runs these are 36%, 57% and 6% of conflict cases. Expected behavior:
> answer with the gold value or flag the conflict. Severity is the number of
> conflicting copies and their position.

## missing_evidence / hard_negative: replaces their description in Section III / Table II

> **missing_evidence.** All evidence for the answer is removed; expected behavior is to
> abstain. Severity is how close the remaining context is to the removed evidence:
> *low* keeps only passages from other pages, *medium* keeps neighboring chunks of the
> source page, and *high* keeps the gold chunk with the supporting sentence deleted.
> **hard_negative.** The gold chunk is replaced by topically similar passages that do not
> contain the answer. For both operators, a case is rejected if any passage still
> contains the gold answer in any surface form (bracket, date-order, comma-head and
> type-suffix variants, matched on whole words). The generator-independent LLM judge
> then checks every remaining case, and the case is rejected if the judge can still
> answer from the contexts. Across the three runs the judge rejects 10–15% of
> high-severity missing_evidence and 3–7% of hard_negative cases that pass the string
> checks; no abstain-expected case is left unaudited.

**Findings paragraph (replaces "Five findings follow from Tables 12–13…"):**

> Five findings follow from Tables XII, XIII, XV and XVI. First, the absence operators
> work as intended: the three models abstain on 93–95% of missing_evidence and 93–94%
> of hard_negative cases. Of the remaining answers, only 0–2% of cases are correct
> (mostly seeds the model answers closed-book: 2.1% vs 0.8% on missing_evidence), and
> 4–7% are hallucinated. The rate of failing to abstain rises with how close the
> remaining context is to the removed evidence: 0.3, 5.6 and 13.5 points at low, medium
> and high severity (Table XVI). Second, context_noise, chunk_boundary and
> evidence_position are weak stressors at pilot severities: paired drops are at most
> 4.5 points. chunk_boundary grows with severity (0.2 / 3.5 / 6.6 points), while
> context_noise and evidence_position show no severity effect: with eight passages,
> these models show neither distractor sensitivity nor positional bias.
> Third, conflict is the dominant failure for every model: paired drops are 27.5 ±
> 1.4 (nova-pro), 23.8 ± 0.7 (llama) and 30.0 ± 1.5 (gpt-oss) points. In 18–27% of
> conflict cases the model commits to the planted alternate without noting the
> contradiction. Fourth, how a model fails conflict depends on the model, not only on
> the operator. Under the default "shortest exact answer" prompt, llama names both
> values in 40% of cases, while nova-pro and gpt-oss pick one value (gold in 57% and
> 48%). A judge that requires a single answer marks many of llama's two-value answers
> wrong, so llama ranks last on judge-only accuracy (0.59) and first on handled rate
> (0.73). For this reason we score conflict by outcome rather than by the judge alone.
> Fifth, conflict behavior depends on the prompt and on prior knowledge. When the
> prompt permits flagging a conflict, acknowledgement rises from 13–41% to 59–85% and
> the handled rate reaches 0.88–0.96. Seeds the model can already answer closed-book
> are handled 14–16 points more often than seeds it cannot (e.g. 81.6% vs 65.5% for
> nova-pro). Part of the default-prompt conflict accuracy is therefore parametric
> recall, not resolution of the retrieved evidence.

## Section VI.B (failure insights): replaces the conflict part

> The larger, more actionable insight is conflict. Gold evidence remains present, in
> full, next to a passage identical except for the answer. Even so, all three models
> lose 24–30 points, and in roughly a quarter of cases they adopt the planted value
> silently. Two controls separate this from artifacts of the operator. A closed-book
> probe shows the models recall 26–44% of the answers without context, and those seeds
> are resolved more often. A conflict-aware prompt shows the models *can* detect the
> contradiction (acknowledgement 59–85%), but the usual "give the shortest answer"
> instruction suppresses it. For a deployed RAG system, this means a stale but
> not-yet-purged document is not something the model can be relied on to adjudicate.
> Whether the contradiction is surfaced depends on the answer-format instruction, and
> which value is chosen depends partly on what the model already believes.

## Section V.H (new): the regression loop on two corpus snapshots

> **Setup.** Snapshot A is the pilot corpus (768 Wikipedia pages fetched on
> 2026-08-01). Snapshot B re-fetches the same pages on 2026-10-28; revision ids are
> recorded, so both snapshots are reproducible. In three months, 580 of 768 pages have
> a new revision and 4 no longer resolve. Of A's 12,366 chunks, 11,296 are unchanged;
> B has 13,599 chunks. Of the 600 clean seeds built on A, 4.0–6.5% no longer hold on B:
> 28 have an edited supporting sentence and 3 have lost the gold answer entirely (e.g.
> a CEO question whose answer changed). A static benchmark therefore goes stale at
> about 1.3–2.2% of seeds per month on this corpus.
>
> **Regeneration.** We rebuilt the benchmark from snapshot B with the unchanged
> pipeline and configuration (seed 42). After fetching and chunking, regeneration took 14 minutes of wall time
> (generation 4.1 min, validation 2.3, seed selection < 1 s, conflict alternates 2.6,
> injection with judge audit 5.1) and 3,736 logged generator / judge calls (1.8M tokens; injection-judge calls are not logged),
> excluding the 3-model evaluation (23,188 calls including judge calls, 13.6M tokens,
> 2.8 h at the gateway's concurrency limit of 10). The funnel reproduces: 1,000
> candidates, 390 accepted, 200 seeds, 3,421 verified failure cases (179 quarantined,
> 0 unaudited). Only 23 of B's 200 questions coincide with A's, so A and B are two
> independent draws of the benchmark.
>
> **Gate.** For each model × condition the gate compares a candidate run with a
> baseline run. It reports a FAIL when the expected-behavior score drops by more than
> 5 points and the 95% bootstrap CI of the drop (2,000 resamples) lies below zero. The
> comparison is paired by item when both runs share a benchmark and unpaired when it
> was regenerated. We run two comparisons (Table XVII):
>
> 1. *Knowledge update, same RAG system* (A → B, unpaired). Every one of the 21 cells
>    passes (largest drop 2.8 points; FRS 0.924/0.939/0.919 on A vs 0.943/0.952/0.925 on
>    B). Decision: SHIP. The corpus update does not by itself degrade the system.
> 2. *Degraded retrieval configuration* (B, top-k 8 → 4, paired). The reader receives
>    only the first four passages. Clean accuracy is unchanged for every model (Δ ≤ 0.5
>    points), because the gold passage is ranked first, so a clean-only regression test
>    would ship this change. The failure operators block it: evidence_position drops
>    64–65 points, context_noise 19–20, conflict 8–14 and chunk_boundary 6–7.5 (all CIs
>    below zero). FRS falls from 0.94–0.95 to 0.76–0.78. The abstain-expected operators
>    are unaffected, as expected, since truncation cannot add evidence. Decision: BLOCK.
>
> The second comparison is the case the benchmark is designed for. A configuration
> error that is invisible on clean questions is caught, and the operators that fail
> name the component: positional and distractor operators point to the
> retrieval-depth setting.

## Response-to-reviewers points (Reviewer #1)

- **Matched parent:** Confirmed, and caused by a stale resume. Generation resumed from a
  checkpoint whose chunks were no longer in the corpus, so some failure cases did not
  match their clean parent. The pipeline now drops stale checkpoints and refuses to
  select seeds from a stale candidate file. All three runs were regenerated from
  scratch, and drops are paired per parent seed.
- **Accuracy vs. abstention:** Abstain-expected operators are now scored on correct
  abstention, not answer accuracy. Previously, missing_evidence and hard_negative
  "accuracy" near zero was the desired behavior shown as a failure.
- **Self-judging:** The earlier judge silently fell back to nova-pro, an evaluated
  model. The judge is now DeepSeek-V3.2, and independence is enforced in code.
- **Silent failures:** Failed calls were scored as wrong answers. They are now excluded
  and counted (0 in the final runs).
- **Untested regeneration claim / no retriever:** Done as suggested: two Wikipedia
  revision snapshots three months apart plus one deliberately degraded retrieval
  configuration (top-k 8 → 4), run with the existing code (Section V.H). The corpus
  update passes the gate; the top-k change keeps clean accuracy unchanged and is
  blocked by four operators. Regeneration costs 14 minutes and about 3.7k LLM calls.
- **Leakage in abstain operators:** Answer surface variants are now matched, and an
  independent judge audits every abstain-expected case. This lowered the
  missing_evidence drop from 9–12 to 5–7 points and made its severity curve monotone.
- **Statistics:** The gate reports bootstrap 95% CIs per cell; seeded-run tables report
  mean ± std over three runs.
- **Judge validation:** A blind 270-answer sheet (15 per model × condition) is in
  `reviews/pilot_stability_s42/`. Cohen's κ is computed by `ragfailbench
  judge-agreement` once it is labeled (*pending*).
