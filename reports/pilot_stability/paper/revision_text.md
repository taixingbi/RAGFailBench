## Section IV (setup): added paragraph after the "two families of experiments" paragraph

> **Scoring.** Each item is scored against its expected behavior. Answer-expected
> items (clean, conflict, context_noise, chunk_boundary, evidence_position) are scored
> on answer correctness: an item is correct if any of exact match, token-F1 ≥ 0.6
> or the LLM-judge verdict accepts it (Table XIX gives each signal alone; the judge
> carries most of the decision, and the lexical signals add about one point on clean).
> Abstain-expected items (missing_evidence, hard_negative) are scored on correct
> abstention. A condition's drop is *paired*: every failure case is compared with
> its own parent clean seed, not with the clean-set mean, so the drop is not
> confounded by which seeds an operator could be built from. The Failure Robustness
> Score is FRS = 1 − mean paired drop over the six operators. The correctness judge
> (DeepSeek-V3.2) is outside the evaluated set; the pipeline refuses to run if the
> judge is also an evaluated model. Calls that fail after retries are excluded and
> counted, never scored as wrong; the final runs have 0 failed calls.
>
> **Fixed context budget.** Every clean and failure case presents exactly eight
> passages. Operators that produce fewer (e.g. missing_evidence keeps only the
> passages left after removal; chunk_boundary uses three to five) are padded with
> off-topic passages from other pages, drawn with a per-case seed and checked not to
> contain any surface form of the answer. A paired drop therefore reflects what an
> operator changes, not a shorter or longer context than the parent clean case.
>
> **Uncertainty.** Besides mean ± std over the three seeded runs, we report 95%
> bootstrap CIs pooled over the runs (2,000 resamples). The resampling unit is the
> parent clean seed (561 clusters), so all failure variants of a seed move together,
> and all models are resampled on the same clusters, which makes model-vs-model FRS
> differences paired.

## Table II / Section III operator descriptions: corrections found by the audit

- **context_noise:** every case has the gold passage and seven others; severity sets how
  many of the seven are topically similar distractors (2 / 4 / 6), the rest being
  off-topic padding. It is not "25 / 50 / 75% noise" of a smaller context.
- **conflict (low):** gold passage in slot 1 and the conflicting copy in slot 2 (then
  padding), not "conflict at the end". Medium: conflicting copy in slot 1, gold in slot
  2. High: conflicting copy in slots 1 and 7, gold in slot 8.
- **missing_evidence (medium):** keeps up to two neighbouring passages of the source page;
  in 59 of 515 medium cases (11%) the page has none, so those cases are effectively
  low severity.

## Conflict operator: replaces its description in Section III / Table II

> **conflict.** The gold chunk is kept, and a second copy of the *same* chunk is
> inserted with the answer span replaced by a plausible alternate. The conflicting
> passage therefore matches the gold passage in length, style and surrounding facts,
> and differs only in the answer. Alternates are chosen in this order:
> (i) for dates and numbers, a small perturbation of the gold value;
> (ii) otherwise, an alternate written by the generator LLM. It must not overlap the
> gold answer, must not already appear in the passage, and is rejected by the
> independent judge if it is judged correct;
> (iii) as a fallback, a same-type entity drawn from other seeds' answers.
> Across the three runs these are 40%, 56% and 4% of conflict cases. Expected behavior:
> answer with the gold value or flag the conflict. Severity is the number of
> conflicting copies and their position.

## missing_evidence / hard_negative: replaces their description in Section III / Table II

> **missing_evidence.** All evidence for the answer is removed; expected behavior is to
> abstain. Severity is how close the remaining context is to the removed evidence:
> *low* keeps only passages from other pages, *medium* keeps neighboring chunks of the
> source page, and *high* keeps the gold chunk with the supporting sentence deleted.
> **hard_negative.** The gold chunk is replaced by topically similar passages that do not
> contain the answer. For both operators, a case is rejected if any passage still
> contains the gold answer in any surface form (bracket, date-order, comma-head and
> type-suffix variants, matched on whole words). The generator-independent LLM judge
> then checks every remaining case, and the case is rejected if the judge can still
> answer from the contexts. Across the three runs the judge rejects 12–20% of
> high-severity and 6–10% of medium-severity missing_evidence cases, and 4–5% of
> hard_negative cases, that pass the string checks; no abstain-expected case is left
> unaudited.

> Five findings follow from Tables XII, XIII and XV–XVII. First, the absence operators
> work as intended: the three models abstain on 92–93% of missing_evidence and 97–98% of
> hard_negative cases. Of the remaining answers, about 1% of cases are correct and
> 6–7% (missing_evidence) or 2% (hard_negative) are hallucinated. The rate of failing
> to abstain rises with how close the remaining context is to the removed evidence:
> 0.0, 7.8 and 15.7 points at low, medium and high severity (Table XVI). Second,
> conflict is the dominant failure for every model: paired drops are 28.5 [25.6, 31.4]
> (nova-pro), 26.6 [24.1, 29.3] (llama) and 30.4 [27.6, 33.3] (gpt-oss) points, rising
> with severity from 12.2 to 28.9 to 44.4 points. The three CIs overlap, so conflict
> exposes a shared weakness rather than a model ranking. In 18–26% of conflict cases
> the model commits to the planted alternate without noting the contradiction. Third,
> context_noise and evidence_position are not stressors for these models with a fixed
> budget of eight passages: every model's 95% CI includes zero, so they show neither
> distractor sensitivity nor positional bias at this length. chunk_boundary grows with
> severity (0.4 / 3.0 / 5.8 points) and separates the models: it costs gpt-oss 5.4
> points [3.8, 7.0] and the others 1.2–1.8. Section V.H shows that the two null
> operators still matter as regression tests: they are the ones that catch a
> retrieval-depth error that clean accuracy misses.
> Fourth, how a model fails conflict depends on the model, not only on the operator.
> Under the default "shortest exact answer" prompt, llama names both values in 35% of
> cases, while nova-pro and gpt-oss usually pick one value (gold in 59% and 50%). A
> judge that requires a single answer marks many of llama's two-value answers wrong,
> so llama ranks last on judge-only accuracy (0.62 vs 0.69 and 0.64) yet first on the
> handled rate (0.72 vs 0.70 and 0.68). For this reason we score conflict by outcome
> rather than by the judge alone. Fifth, conflict behavior depends on the prompt and on
> prior knowledge. When the prompt permits flagging a conflict, acknowledgement rises
> from 11–35% to 59–85% and the handled rate reaches 0.89–0.96. Seeds the model can
> already answer closed-book are handled 11–23 points more often than seeds it cannot
> (e.g. 86.5% vs 63.2% for nova-pro). Part of the default-prompt conflict accuracy is
> therefore parametric recall, not resolution of the retrieved evidence.

## Section VI.B (failure insights): replaces the conflict part

> The larger, more actionable insight is conflict. Gold evidence remains present, in
> full, next to a passage identical except for the answer. Even so, all three models
> lose 27–30 points, and in a fifth to a quarter of cases they adopt the planted value
> silently. The weakness is shared: no model is significantly better than both others.
> Two controls separate this from artifacts of the operator. A closed-book probe shows
> the models recall 28–49% of the answers without context, and those seeds are resolved
> more often. A conflict-aware prompt shows the models *can* detect the contradiction
> (acknowledgement 59–85%), but the usual "give the shortest answer" instruction
> suppresses it. For a deployed RAG system, this means a stale but not-yet-purged
> document is not something the model can be relied on to adjudicate. Whether the
> contradiction is surfaced depends on the answer-format instruction, and which value
> is chosen depends partly on what the model already believes.

## Section V.H (new): the regression loop on two corpus snapshots

> **Setup.** Snapshot A is the pilot corpus (768 Wikipedia pages fetched on
> 2026-08-01). Snapshot B re-fetches the same pages on 2026-10-28; revision ids are
> recorded, so both snapshots are reproducible. In three months, 580 of 768 pages have
> a new revision and 4 no longer resolve. Of A's 12,366 chunks, 11,296 are unchanged;
> B has 13,599 chunks. Of the 600 clean seeds built on A, 4.0–6.5% no longer hold on B:
> 28 have an edited supporting sentence and 3 have lost the gold answer entirely (e.g.
> a CEO question whose answer changed). A static benchmark therefore goes stale at
> about 1.3–2.2% of seeds per month on this corpus.
>
> **Regeneration.** We rebuilt the benchmark from snapshot B with the unchanged
> pipeline and configuration (seed 42). After fetching and chunking, regeneration
> (generation, validation, seed selection, conflict alternates, injection with judge
> audit) took 18 minutes of wall time and about 4.9k logged generator / judge calls
> (injection-judge calls are not logged), excluding the 3-model evaluation (3.3 h at
> the gateway's concurrency limit of 10). Ingest is not the bottleneck: a timed
> re-fetch of the 768 pages (764 resolve, 11 MB of wikitext) takes 188 s at the
> configured 8 requests/s API limit (4.1 pages/s), and chunking them into 13.6k chunks
> takes 16 s on one CPU core. End to end, a regeneration is about 22 minutes; its cost
> is dominated by the evaluation, which scales with models × cases, not with corpus
> size. The funnel reproduces: 1,000 candidates, 769 accepted, 200 seeds (188 after
> the multi-part filter), 3,204 verified failure cases (0 unaudited). Only 18 of B's
> 200 selected questions coincide with A's, so A and B are two independent draws of
> the benchmark.
>
> **Gate.** For each model × condition the gate compares a candidate run with a
> baseline run. It reports a FAIL when the expected-behavior score drops by more than
> 5 points and the 95% bootstrap CI of the drop (2,000 resamples) lies below zero. The
> comparison is paired by item when both runs share a benchmark and unpaired when it
> was regenerated. We run two comparisons (Table XVIII):
>
> 1. *Knowledge update, same RAG system* (A → B, unpaired). Every one of the 21 cells
>    passes (largest drop 1.5 points, every CI includes zero; FRS 0.935/0.945/0.924 on
>    A vs 0.938/0.955/0.928 on B for nova-pro/llama/gpt-oss). Decision: SHIP. The
>    corpus update does not by itself degrade the system, and regenerating the
>    benchmark does not raise a false alarm.
> 2. *Degraded retrieval configuration* (B, top-k 8 → 4, paired). The reader receives
>    only the first four of the eight passages. Clean accuracy is unchanged for every
>    model (Δ between 0 and +1.6 points), because the gold passage is ranked first, so
>    a clean-only regression test would ship this change. The failure operators block
>    it in 12 of 21 cells: evidence_position drops 65–66 points, context_noise 46–47,
>    conflict 8–14 and chunk_boundary 8–10 (all CIs below zero). context_noise places
>    the gold passage uniformly over the eight slots and evidence_position at slot 1,
>    5 or 8 (by severity), so a top-4 cut removes it in about half and two thirds of
>    cases; the scores (≈0.51 and ≈0.32) match the evidence the configuration
>    discards. FRS falls from 0.93–0.96 to 0.71–0.72. The abstain-expected operators
>    are unaffected, as expected, since truncation cannot add evidence. Decision:
>    BLOCK.
>
> The second comparison is the case the benchmark is designed for. A configuration
> error that is invisible on clean questions is caught, and the operators that fail
> name the component: positional and distractor operators, which show no effect on a
> correctly configured system, point to the retrieval-depth setting.

## Section III.C (validation): rule definitions and quality score (Reviewer #3)

> **Rule checks.** A candidate is rejected by any of the following deterministic
> checks. Matching is on normalized text (lower-cased, punctuation and articles
> removed) unless stated otherwise. "Only this rule" counts candidates rejected by
> that rule and by no other rule, judge or baseline check.
>
> | Rule | Rejects when | Example rejected | Rejected / run | Only this rule |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | possible_multiple_answers (uniqueness) | a *who/which/what* question with a person or organization answer, whose supporting sentence lists entities joined by "and" that the gold answer does not | "Who was responsible for the urban planning of Brasília?" (gold: Lúcio Costa; sentence names Costa, Oscar Niemeyer and Joaquim Cardozo) | 90.3 | 74.0 |
> | answer_not_in_supporting_sentence (containment) | the gold answer does not occur in the supporting sentence | "In the example provided, how many phases are present in a system consisting of ice, water, and humid air in a glass jar?" | 54.7 | 44.3 |
> | refers_to_passage | the question points at its source ("the passage", "the text", "mentioned") | "What is the population of the Metropolis GZM conurbation as mentioned in the passage?" | 38.0 | 32.0 |
> | time_sensitive_phrasing | relative time ("current", "latest", "recently", an undated "as of") | "Where is International Automated Systems currently based?" | 16.3 | 14.0 |
> | answer_in_question | the gold answer occurs in the question | "What was the name of the settlement … captured by forces of the Demak Sultanate in 1527 and later renamed Jayakarta?" (gold: Jayakarta) | 10.3 | 8.0 |
> | unresolved_referent | a pronoun with no name before it, or a possessive / "the company"-style phrase in a question that names nothing | "What is the location of the company that develops industry-standard validation tools for development, certification, and manufacturing?" | 10.3 | 8.0 |
> | supporting_sentence_not_in_chunk (containment) | the supporting sentence is not a substring of the source chunk | "What phenomenon did Louis Néel suggest about 1930…?" (sentence not found in the cited chunk) | 7.0 | 4.0 |
> | title_leak_in_question | the question contains the page title and no other content word, i.e. it only asks what the title is | "What is a sunset provision?" (page *Sunset provision*) | 3.3 | 3.3 |
> | multi_part_question | "and" followed by a wh-word ("when and where", "…, and what…"), or a question opening "wh- or wh-" | "When and where did Ray Dolby found Dolby Laboratories?" (gold: 1965) | 95 of 1,000 candidates (s42) | applied to selected seeds: 11–16 per run (Table V) |
> | length / empty | question < 12 characters or > 60 tokens; empty question or answer | — | 0 | 0 |
>
> Naming the subject is required for a question to be self-contained, so a title or
> entity name in the question is not by itself a leak; only questions that ask for
> the title's own definition are rejected. The referent and time rules are
> deliberately conservative and also reject some acceptable questions (e.g. "…change
> its name from Apple Computer, Inc. to its current name?"); we accept this loss of
> recall for precision. The time rule matches relative-time words, not tense, so
present-tense facts that can change ("Where is Intuit Inc. headquartered?") pass it;
the human audit finds two such seeds in 50 (Section V.E). The multi-part rule was added after the runs and applied to
> the selected seeds (Table V); in the released pipeline it runs with the other rules.
> Schema validation parses the generator's JSON against the CandidateQA schema (100%
> pass). Deduplication runs last over accepted candidates and drops a candidate whose
> normalized question, or normalized (supporting sentence, answer) pair, matches an
> earlier one exactly or has character-5-gram Jaccard similarity ≥ 0.85 (2.3 per run).
>
> **Quality score.** q = 0.30·[rules pass] + 0.10·[uniqueness passes]
> + 0.30·c·(a + s + u + k)/4 + 0.30·max(F1_baseline, [baseline correct]), where
> a, s, u, k are the judge's answerable / supported / unique / clear verdicts and
> c its confidence. In the released configuration the threshold of 0.85 is
> redundant: every candidate that passes the hard gates (rules, uniqueness, judge
> with confidence ≥ 0.6, baseline correct) scores ≥ 0.985; a rule failure caps q at
> 0.70 and a uniqueness failure at 0.90. Sweeping the threshold from 0 to 0.95 accepts
> exactly the same 750.0 candidates per run (747.7 after deduplication). We therefore
> describe q as a ranking score reported with each seed, not as a separate filtering
> stage, and drop the "quality threshold" row from Table VII (its 170 rejections are
> all also rejected by another signal).
